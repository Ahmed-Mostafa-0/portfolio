    Project 1

    Tomato sauce HACCP and ISO 22000 case study

    A food safety system for a tomato sauce manufacturing and packaging process, built on HACCP and ISO 22000 principles. It covers hazards, preventive controls, critical control points, monitoring, corrective action and verification.

    • Raw material receiving
    • Processing and preparation
    • Cooking and heat treatment
    • Filling
    • Packaging
    • Finished product storage

    Hazard analysis

    Process stepHazardTypeControl measureSignificant
    ReceivingPathogenic microorganismsBiologicalApproved suppliers and inspectionYes
    ReceivingPesticide residuesChemicalSupplier control and specificationsYes
    ProcessingSurvival of microorganismsBiologicalControlled heat treatmentYes
    FillingRecontaminationBiologicalHygienic filling conditionsYes
    PackagingForeign bodyPhysicalPackaging inspectionYes
    • Identify biological, chemical and physical hazards at each step.
    • Assess significance with the selected risk assessment approach.
    • Define preventive or control measures.
    • Document the rationale for hazards that need further control.

    Critical control points

    A documented decision process decides whether a step needs control as a CCP. It weighs the hazard, the control measure, how likely the hazard is, and whether control is essential at that step.

    CCPHazardCritical limitMonitoringCorrective action
    Cooking CCPMicrobial survivalValidated time/temperature limit defined for the processTemperature and timeStop the process and evaluate the product
    Filling CCPRecontaminationValidated hygienic limits defined for the filling operationPeriodic monitoringCorrect the condition and assess the product

    No numeric limits are stated here. They must be validated against the product, the process, legal requirements, scientific references and site-specific data.

    Control cycle

    1. Define critical limit
    2. Monitor and record
    3. Identify deviation
    4. Correct and control product
    5. Verify
    MonitoringWhat is measured, how, when and by whom. Records give objective evidence of control.
    Corrective actionIdentify the deviation, control the affected product, restore the process, find the cause and document what was done.
    VerificationReview records, confirm monitoring took place, check equipment calibration where it applies, and review how well the controls work.

    Traceability

    1. Supplier
    2. Raw material lot
    3. Production date
    4. Production batch
    5. Packaging
    6. Finished product
    7. Customer
    • Track raw materials and finished products by lot.
    • Link production records to specific lots and batches.
    • Support complaint and nonconformity investigations, and targeted withdrawal or recall.
    • Cover one step back and one step forward, with defined records and responsibilities.

    Emergency preparedness plan
